New Jersey's Transit Village Initiative began in 1999 with five municipalities: Morristown, Pleasantville, Rutherford, South Amboy, and South Orange.

Four were rail-served. Pleasantville was centered on a bus terminal. Two of the five share NJ TRANSIT's Morristown Line.

01 / FINDING
What the record gives us

Five places
came first.
They were not
all the same.

On March 4, 1999, New Jersey announced the launch of its Transit Village Program. The announcement said that five communities had already started discussions about implementing the initiative: Morristown, Pleasantville, Rutherford, South Amboy, and South Orange.

The original announcement described the program in strongly passenger-rail terms. But the founding cohort itself was more varied: four municipalities were rail-served, while Pleasantville was centered on a bus terminal.

That difference is not an explanation. It is an observation worth placing next to the rest of the record.

CLAIM BOUNDARY

The public record identifies the founding five. It does not, in the sources reviewed for this edition, provide a complete explanation for why these five came first.

04 / RELATIONSHIP
A documented overlap

Two places.
One named line.

● OBSERVED

Both municipalities belong to the 1999 founding cohort. NJ TRANSIT's current Morristown Line listing includes both Morristown and South Orange.

Put those two confirmed facts next to each other and a transit-network overlap becomes visible inside the founding cohort.

THIS IS NOT THE EXPLANATION

No reviewed source establishes that their shared rail line influenced the state's decision to designate either municipality.

08 / BOUNDARY
What this does not prove

Evidence has
edges.

ESTABLISHED

  • Five municipalities were named at the 1999 launch.
  • Four were rail-served; Pleasantville had a bus-terminal-centered context.
  • Morristown and South Orange share the Morristown Line.
  • Four founding municipalities were listed in NJTPA; Pleasantville in SJTPO.
  • NJDOT currently lists 37 designated Transit Villages.

NOT ESTABLISHED

  • That the shared Morristown Line influenced the 1999 designations.
  • That Pleasantville was selected as an intentional exception.
  • That the MPO split influenced the selection.
  • That the founding five were chosen for a single common reason.
  • That later designation cohorts reproduce the same relationship.
09 / OPEN QUESTION

WHY
THESE FIVE?

The March 4, 1999 announcement tells us something unusually useful: the five communities were already discussing implementation when the state announced the program.

But identifying the participants is not the same thing as identifying the selection rationale.

The next evidence is therefore not another visualization. It is the underlying historical record.
